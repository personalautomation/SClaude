# Two pieces of advice

*Just so you can skip our mistakes, whether you use SClaude or not.*

## 1. Build feedback loops into your orchestrator

A process only works if it's comfortable; otherwise people work around it. And
without a process there is no data, and without data no improvement.

- **Change the process quickly** when something gets in the way. A friction
  review after every step lets you start tight without fighting the tool.
- **Count before you decide.** One refusal is an anecdote; the same one in five
  features is a pattern. Decide on the count, not on the best story.
- **Let people learn from their own run.** A retrospective after each feature,
  facts not opinions, with nobody else judging.
- **Leave room for personal add-ons** on top of the core process, and let a
  lesson climb — from one person, to the company, to the open-source tool — as
  far as it proves itself.
- **Collect metrics without overhead.** Not a single field in Jira. If
  collecting a metric needs an action from a developer, don't.

See [Feedback Loops](Feedback-Loops).

## 2. Give the team one shared tool, and let everyone contribute

Ask everyone to put their tools into the company's git, as they are — a
bicycle, a skateboard, whatever they built. If everyone is ashamed of the same
thing, you get support, not shame.

To make one tool fit everybody's needs, people have to talk: *why do you do it
this way?* While they argue and agree, their processes line up.

**Before, a zoo of bicycles: each rides wherever it likes, but slowly. Now,
cars on a road: there are rules, but everyone goes much faster.** And the
company is one team again.

See [Why SClaude](Why-SClaude).

## And one principle under both

**Don't make the model the process engine.** Whatever repeats or has to scale
goes into scripts; limits go into code, not prompts. Thinking stays with AI.

> AI speed is easy to buy. Control is what has to be designed.
