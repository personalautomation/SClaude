Rules in a prompt are wishes. Rules in a hook, plus a review of what they caught, are a process.

10
This is the part people underestimate. Anyone can write a list of banned commands. The know-how is the second half: reading the refusals after every step, deciding case by case whether the ban protected us or slowed us down, and making that decision once, in code, for the whole team.
Where tokens go

Pay for thinking, not for running errands

The orchestrator does this for free

  • gathers the issue, its parents and the manual sections that matter
  • builds each step's prompt from templates
  • sets up sandboxes, dev servers and databases
  • runs regressions and deploys; a session opens only when something is red
  • keeps the issue and the board up to date
  • runs on the team's own machines: no need to pay AI for it
